N°1 · Cosmetics

Do peptides actually do anything to your skin?

Verdict from a 2026 meta-analysis (19 RCTs, 1,341 participants):

Hydration✓ SIGNIFICANT
Brightness✓ SIGNIFICANT
Wrinkle reduction~ MODEST
Elasticity✗ NOT PROVEN

The claim marketing pushes hardest — elasticity — is the least supported by the pooled data. Oral formulations consistently beat creams.

Source: Nukaly et al., Frontiers in Medicine, 17 March 2026 (doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1618306).

Our editorial rule

"Written" is not "proven."

What this newsletter will never do

The web is saturated with peptides sold as miracles by influencers. We take the opposite path — and we own it:

  • No figure gets published without its primary source cited.
  • An effect that's "significant in marketing" but weak in pooled analysis is labelled as such.
  • Never any promotion of unregulated injectable peptides (BPC-157, TB-500 & co. have no rigorous human safety data).
  • No medical advice. For any therapeutic molecule: medical consultation, full stop.
